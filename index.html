What We Didn't Know

I run sessions where people share, listen, and notice — in pairs, then in groups. Connection first. Collaboration follows.

People learn things about each other
they wouldn't have found out otherwise.

The story I keep coming back to

I ran a session for 30 people at my own organization. By the end, the room felt like they really knew each other — not "we did an icebreaker" knew each other, but knew each other. People learned things about each other they never would have found out otherwise.

That's the whole point.

What happens in the room

Simple on purpose.

  1. Pairs

    People partner up and share. Listening is the work.

  2. Noticing

    We stop and ask: what did you notice? In your partner, in yourself, in the room?

  3. Groups

    We bring it back to the whole room — what we heard, what surprised us, what we didn't know.

  4. Repeat

    Each round goes a little deeper, because trust is already there.

Sessions run from 90 minutes to a half-day, and can be woven into a larger event. Worked with groups up to 30; comfortable up to 100. I travel, and I facilitate online.

What I'm drawing on

I'm a Certified Interplay Leader. My work is informed by Interplay's ideas about story and play, Tim Gallwey's Inner Game approach to noticing what's actually happening, and Peter Block's thinking on community and belonging.

I hold these ideas proudly — and I hold them loosely. The session is not a lecture about any of them. It's an experience of them.
